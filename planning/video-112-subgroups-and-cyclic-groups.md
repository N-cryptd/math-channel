# Video 112: Subgroups and Cyclic Groups

## Target Audience
Undergraduate Abstract Algebra students

## Learning Objectives
- Define subgroup and give examples.
- Understand cyclic groups and generators.
- Prove that a subset is a subgroup using the subgroup test.
- Find the order of an element and the subgroup it generates.
- Understand that every subgroup of a cyclic group is cyclic.

## Key Definitions
- **Subgroup**: A subset H of a group G that is itself a group under the operation of G.
- **Cyclic group**: A group that can be generated by a single element.
- **Generator**: An element g such that every element of the group is a power of g.
- **Order of an element**: The smallest positive integer n such that g^n = e (identity).

## Key Theorems/Propositions
- **Subgroup test**: A non-empty subset H of a group G is a subgroup if for all a,b in H, ab^{-1} is in H.
- Every cyclic group is abelian.
- Every subgroup of a cyclic group is cyclic.
- If G is a cyclic group of order n, then for each divisor d of n, there is exactly one subgroup of order d.

## Examples
- The set of even integers is a subgroup of (Z, +).
- The set of rotations of a regular n-gon forms a cyclic subgroup of the dihedral group D_n.
- The group (Z_n, +) is cyclic, generated by 1 (or any integer coprime to n).
- The group of units modulo n, (Z_n^x, *,) is not always cyclic (e.g., n=8).

## Visualizations
- Cayley table for small groups (e.g., Z_2, Z_3).
- Cycle graphs for cyclic groups (showing the cyclic nature).
- Diagram of subgroup lattice for cyclic groups (e.g., Z_12 showing subgroups of orders 1,2,3,4,6,12).
- Animation of generating a cyclic group by repeatedly applying the generator (e.g., adding 1 mod n).
- Visualization of the subgroup test: checking if a subset is closed under the operation and inverses.
