# Video 07: Trigonometric Derivatives
## Overview
- **Topic**: Derivatives of sin(x), cos(x), tan(x), and the other trig functions
- **Hook**: "What's the derivative of sin(x)? It's cos(x)... but why?"
- **Aha moment**: The geometric proof using the unit circle and squeeze theorem
- **Target audience**: Watched Videos 01-06 (know chain rule, product rule)
- **Length**: ~12 min

## Scenes
1. **Hook** — "What's the derivative of sin(x)?" Show the limit: lim_{h→0} (sin(x+h)-sin(x))/h. Two special limits needed: sin(h)/h → 1 and (cos(h)-1)/h → 0. (~60s)
2. **The two special limits** — Unit circle diagram: show sin(h)/h → 1 geometrically with the sector area argument. Show (cos(h)-1)/h → 0 by manipulation. (~90s)
3. **Proof of sin'(x) = cos(x)** — Using trig identity sin(x+h)=sin(x)cos(h)+cos(x)sin(h), compute the limit: sin'(x)=sin(x)·0+cos(x)·1=cos(x). (~60s)
4. **Proof of cos'(x) = -sin(x)** — Similar: cos(x+h)=cos(x)cos(h)-sin(x)sin(h). Result: cos'(x)=cos(x)·0 - sin(x)·1 = -sin(x). (~60s)
5. **tan(x), sec(x), etc.** — Use quotient rule: tan'(x)=sec²(x), sec'(x)=sec(x)tan(x), cot'(x)=-csc²(x), csc'(x)=-csc(x)cot(x). (~60s)
6. **Examples** — d/dx[sin(3x)] = 3cos(3x) (chain rule), d/dx[x²·sin(x)] = 2x·sin(x) + x²·cos(x). d/dx[tan(2x+1)] = 2·sec²(2x+1). (~60s)
7. **Recap** — Summary table of trig derivatives. "Next: Applications of derivatives — optimization." (~45s)
