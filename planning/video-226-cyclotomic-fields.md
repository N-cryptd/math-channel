## Competitive Analysis

**Key competitors:**
1. **Mathemaniac** -- "Why you can't solve quintic equations" -- mentions cyclotomic extensions briefly as a concrete example of radical extensions.
2. **Michael Penn** -- 'Cyclotomic fields' lecture (~20-30K views). Whiteboard, covers n-th cyclotomic polynomial, irreducibility, Galois group computation. Dense, no animation.
3. **Dr. Peyam** -- 'Cyclotomic Fields' (~15K views). Conversational, accessible, covers basics well but doesn't reach Galois group structure.
4. **Aleph 0** -- 'What is the square root of two?' -- uses Q(√2) as hook, implicitly covers the quadratic cyclotomic case.

**Market gap:** No animated video provides a systematic treatment of cyclotomic fields with full rigor. Lecture channels have the content but no visuals. We fill this by combining animation with the formal treatment.

**Our positioning:** A natural next topic after Abel-Ruffini. The audience just learned that x^5-1 IS solvable despite the general quintic not being solvable. This video explains WHY, through the beautiful structure of cyclotomic fields.

**Techniques to adopt:**
- Visual: show the n-th roots of unity as points on the unit circle, animated
- Build the cyclotomic polynomial step by step: factor x^n - 1 into cyclotomic polynomials
- Show the Galois group (Z/nZ)* explicitly for small examples
- Connect to constructibility (which regular polygons are constructible?)

**Techniques to avoid:**
- Full proof of cyclotomic polynomial irreducibility (cite, motivate, give the key idea)
- Overloading with Dirichlet characters or p-adic valuations
- Skipping the unit circle visualization (this is the key visual hook)

## Scene Plan (8 scenes)

### Scene 1: Hook -- Roots of Unity on the Circle (60s)
**Content budget:** intro + title + unit circle visual + progressive items
**Narration (~30s):** "The solutions to x to the n equals 1 are called the n-th roots of unity. On the complex plane, they sit perfectly spaced on the unit circle, forming a regular n-gon. The field generated by these roots is called a cyclotomic field. These fields are among the most beautiful and well-understood objects in all of algebra. Today we explore their structure, their Galois groups, and their deep connections to number theory."

- play_intro("Cyclotomic Fields", "Advanced Abstract Algebra")
- Title: "Roots of Unity"
- Unit circle with 8th roots of unity plotted (PRIMARY dots, DIM circle)
- 3 progressive items:
  1. "x^n = 1 has n solutions on the unit circle"
  2. "The field Q(zeta_n) is called a cyclotomic field"
  3. "Their Galois groups, polynomials, and connections to number theory"

### Scene 2: Definition and Primitives (70s)
**Content budget:** section divider + title + definition + properties
**Narration (~35s):** "A primitive n-th root of unity is an n-th root of unity that is NOT a k-th root of unity for any proper divisor k of n. The standard notation is zeta sub n equals e to the 2 pi i over n. This is always primitive. For example, when n equals 6, the primitive 6th roots are e to the 2 pi i over 6 and e to the 10 pi i over 6, but e to the 2 pi i over 3 is NOT primitive for n equals 6 because it's already a 3rd root of unity. There are exactly phi of n primitive n-th roots, where phi is Euler's totient function."

- Section divider: "1 -- Roots of Unity"
- Title: "Primitive Roots of Unity"
- Definition: zeta is primitive for n if zeta^k != 1 for all 1 <= k < n
- Standard primitive root: zeta_n = e^{2πi/n}
- Euler's totient: exactly φ(n) primitive roots

### Scene 3: Cyclotomic Polynomials (80s)
**Content budget:** section divider + title + definition + factorization examples
**Narration (~40s):** "The n-th cyclotomic polynomial is defined as the product of x minus zeta, where zeta ranges over all primitive n-th roots of unity. It has integer coefficients. The key identity is: x to the n minus 1 equals the product over all divisors d of n of Phi sub d of x. For small values: Phi sub 1 is x minus 1, Phi sub 2 is x plus 1, Phi sub 3 is x squared plus x plus 1, Phi sub 4 is x squared plus 1, Phi sub 6 is x squared minus x plus 1. Notice that Phi sub n is irreducible over Q for every n. This is a theorem of Gauss."

- Section divider: "2 -- Cyclotomic Polynomials"
- Title: "The n-th Cyclotomic Polynomial"
- Definition box: Phi_n(x) = prod(x - zeta) over primitive n-th roots
- Key identity: x^n - 1 = prod_d Phi_d(x)
- Small examples: Phi_1 through Phi_6
- Note: Phi_n is irreducible over Q (Gauss)

### Scene 4: Cyclotomic Extensions (70s)
**Content budget:** title + degree formula + example
**Narration (~35s):** "The n-th cyclotomic field is Q of zeta sub n. Since Phi sub n is irreducible and has degree phi of n, we have that Q of zeta sub n over Q has degree phi of n. For example, Q of zeta sub 5 has degree phi of 5 equals 4. And Q of zeta sub 8 has degree phi of 8 equals 4. Cyclotomic extensions are Galois extensions because they contain all the roots of x to the n minus 1."

- Title: "Cyclotomic Extensions"
- Degree: [Q(zeta_n) : Q] = φ(n)
- Examples: [Q(zeta_5):Q] = 4, [Q(zeta_8):Q] = 4
- Note: "Always Galois (splitting field of x^n - 1)"

### Scene 5: The Galois Group (80s)
**Content budget:** section divider + title + theorem + small examples
**Narration (~40s):** "Now the beautiful part. The Galois group of the n-th cyclotomic extension over Q is isomorphic to the multiplicative group of integers modulo n. Concretely, each element of Z mod n star sends zeta sub n to zeta sub n to the k-th power. The group has order phi of n. For n equals 5, the Galois group is Z mod 5 star, which is cyclic of order 4. For n equals 8, the Galois group is Z mod 8 star, which is isomorphic to Z over 2 cross Z over 2. For n equals p a prime, the group is cyclic of order p minus 1."

- Section divider: "3 -- Galois Groups"
- Title: "The Galois Group of Q(zeta_n)/Q"
- Theorem box: Gal(Q(zeta_n)/Q) ≅ (Z/nZ)*
- Action: sigma_k(zeta_n) = zeta_n^k
- Examples: n=5 -> Z/5Z* ≅ C4; n=8 -> Z/8Z* ≅ C2 × C2; n=p -> C_{p-1}

### Scene 6: Applications (75s)
**Content budget:** section divider + title + 3 applications
**Narration (~38s):** "Cyclotomic fields have deep applications. First, constructibility. A regular n-gon is constructible with straightedge and compass if and only if phi of n is a power of 2. This means the Galois group must be a 2-group. Gauss famously proved the 17-gon is constructible because phi of 17 equals 16 equals 2 to the 4th. Second, quadratic reciprocity. The law of quadratic reciprocity has a clean proof using cyclotomic fields and the Frobenius element. Third, Fermat's last theorem. Kummer's work on cyclotomic fields led to the first major results toward FLT, introducing the concept of ideal numbers, the precursor to ideals."

- Section divider: "4 -- Applications"
- Title: "Applications of Cyclotomic Fields"
- 3 progressive items:
  1. "Constructibility: regular n-gon constructible iff φ(n) is a power of 2"
  2. "Quadratic reciprocity: clean proof via cyclotomic fields"
  3. "Fermat's Last Theorem: Kummer's ideal numbers from cyclotomic fields"

### Scene 7: Connection to Abel-Ruffini (55s)
**Content budget:** title + 2 items
**Narration (~28s):** "Recall from Video 225 that x to the 5th minus 1 is solvable by radicals, even though the general quintic is not. Now we can see why. The Galois group of Q of zeta sub 5 over Q is cyclic of order 4. Cyclic groups are trivially solvable. So by our solvability theorem, x to the 5th minus 1 is solvable by radicals. More generally, every cyclotomic polynomial is solvable by radicals because its Galois group, Z mod n star, is always abelian, hence solvable."

- Title: "Connection to Abel-Ruffini"
- Item 1: x^5 - 1 has Galois group C4 (abelian, solvable!)
- Item 2: "Every cyclotomic polynomial is solvable by radicals (Galois group is abelian)"

### Scene 8: Summary and Outro (50s)
**Content budget:** section divider + title + 5 takeaways + outro
**Narration (~28s):** "Let's recap. The n-th roots of unity sit on the unit circle. Primitive roots generate the cyclotomic field Q of zeta sub n. The cyclotomic polynomial Phi sub n has degree phi of n and is irreducible. The Galois group is Z mod n star, acting by exponentiation. Cyclotomic fields connect to constructibility, reciprocity, and Fermat's last theorem. In the next video, we explore finite fields."

- Section divider: "5 -- Summary"
- Title: "Key Takeaways"
- 5 progressive items:
  1. "n-th roots of unity: solutions to x^n = 1 on the unit circle"
  2. "Cyclotomic polynomial Φ_n(x): product over primitive roots, irreducible over Q"
  3. "[Q(ζ_n):Q] = φ(n) (Euler's totient)"
  4. "Gal(Q(ζ_n)/Q) ≅ (Z/nZ)*, acting by exponentiation"
  5. "Applications: constructibility, quadratic reciprocity, Fermat's Last Theorem"
