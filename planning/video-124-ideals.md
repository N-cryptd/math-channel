## Scene Plan

### Scene 1: Hook — "Why Not Just Subrings?" (~30s narration)
**Content budget:**
- Title: "Why Not Just Subrings?"
- 3 bullet points about subring limitation
- 1 formula: the failed quotient attempt

**Narration beats:**
- Every ring has subrings, but subrings don't let us form quotient rings
- For groups, we needed NORMAL subgroups. For rings, we need IDEALS
- Ideals are the ring-theoretic analog of normal subgroups

### Scene 2: Motivation — From Normal Subgroups to Ideals (~45s narration)
**Content budget:**
- Section divider: "1 — From Groups to Rings"
- Title: "The Normal Subgroup Analogy"
- 2-column layout: left = groups (normal subgroup → quotient group), right = rings (? → quotient ring)
- Formula: absorption condition as the key requirement

**Narration beats:**
- Review: N ◁ G ↔ gNg^{-1} = N ↔ gN = Ng
- For rings: we need R/I to be a ring. Addition is fine (I is an additive subgroup). Multiplication is the constraint.
- The absorption law: r ∈ R, a ∈ I → ra ∈ I and ar ∈ I
- This is the ring-theoretic analog of normality

### Scene 3: Definition of an Ideal (~40s narration)
**Content budget:**
- Section divider: "2 — Definition"
- Formal definition (MathTex in formula box)
- Two conditions: (I,+) subgroup, absorption law
- Note: left/right/two-sided ideals

**Narration beats:**
- Definition: I ⊆ R is an ideal if (I,+) ≤ (R,+) and ∀ r∈R, a∈I: ra, ar ∈ I
- Notation: I ◁ R (left ideal if only ra∈I, right ideal if only ar∈I)
- Commutative rings: left = right = two-sided automatically
- Every ideal is a subring, but not every subring is an ideal

### Scene 4: Examples (~50s narration)
**Content budget:**
- Example 1: nZ ⊂ Z (progressive reveal)
- Example 2: (x) ⊂ Z[x] — polynomials with zero constant term
- Example 3: {0} and R are trivial ideals
- Counterexample: Z ⊂ Q is a subring but NOT an ideal

**Narration beats:**
- nZ = {…, -2n, -n, 0, n, 2n, …} is an ideal of Z
- For nZ: closed under +, and Z·(nZ) = nZ (absorption)
- In Z[x]: (x) = {x·f(x) | f(x) ∈ Z[x]} — polynomials divisible by x
- Z ⊂ Q is NOT an ideal: 1/2 ∈ Q, 1 ∈ Z, but 1/2·1 = 1/2 ∉ Z

### Scene 5: Principal Ideals and Ideal Operations (~50s narration)
**Content budget:**
- Section divider: "3 — Principal Ideals"
- Definition: (a) = {ra | r ∈ R}
- Examples: (6) = 6Z, (x²) in Z[x]
- Visual: containment diagram for Z ideals
- Ideal sum: I + J = {i + j | i∈I, j∈J}
- Product and intersection (brief)

**Narration beats:**
- A principal ideal is generated by a single element: (a) = {ra + na | r ∈ R, n ∈ Z}
- In Z: every ideal is principal! (Z is a PID)
- Containment: (a) ⊆ (b) iff b | a
- Ideal sum I+J is the smallest ideal containing both I and J
- Intersection I∩J is also an ideal

### Scene 6: Prime and Maximal Ideals (~45s narration)
**Content budget:**
- Section divider: "4 — Prime and Maximal Ideals"
- Prime ideal definition with condition: ab ∈ I → a∈I or b∈I
- Maximal ideal definition: no proper ideal strictly between I and R
- Venn diagram metaphor: prime as "no zero divisors", maximal as "no room above"
- Z example: (p) is prime/maximal for prime p

**Narration beats:**
- Prime ideal P: ab ∈ P → a ∈ P or b ∈ P (like primality of integers)
- Maximal ideal M: if M ⊆ J ⊆ R and J is ideal, then J = M or J = R
- Every maximal ideal is prime, but not conversely (unless R is a PID)
- In Z: (p) is both prime and maximal when p is prime
- In Z[x]: (x) is prime but not maximal (since (x) ⊂ (2, x) ⊂ Z[x])

### Scene 7: Key Theorems (~45s narration)
**Content budget:**
- Section divider: "5 — Key Theorems"
- Theorem 1: P is prime ↔ R/P is an integral domain (formula box)
- Theorem 2: M is maximal ↔ R/M is a field (formula box)
- Visual: containment chain: field ⊂ integral domain ⊂ ring
- Z_n example: Z/6Z ≅ Z_6 has zero divisors → (6) not prime

**Narration beats:**
- The payoff of studying ideals: they classify quotient rings
- R/P is an integral domain iff P is prime (no zero divisors mirrors the prime condition)
- R/M is a field iff M is maximal (every nonzero element invertible means nothing between M and R)
- Example: Z/(6) ≅ Z_6 has zero divisors (2·3 = 0), so (6) is not prime
- Z/(5) ≅ Z_5 is a field, so (5) is maximal (and prime)

### Scene 8: Summary and Preview (~30s narration)
**Content budget:**
- 4-item summary list (progressive reveal)
- Connection to next video: quotient rings
- Outro with next video card: "Quotient Rings"

**Narration beats:**
- Summary: ideals generalize normal subgroups to rings
- The absorption law is what makes quotient rings possible
- Principal, prime, and maximal ideals reveal ring structure
