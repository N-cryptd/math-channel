## Competitive Analysis Summary

**Market gap:** No major animation channel covers the quotient topology with Manim animations. The concept of "gluing" spaces together is inherently visual — perfect for animation. The torus as a quotient of the square, the Klein bottle, the projective plane, and real projective space are all classic examples that benefit enormously from visual treatment.

**Our opportunity:** First high-production animated treatment. Visual-first approach with:
- Animated "gluing" of edges to form torus/Klein bottle
- Square with arrows showing identification
- Quotient maps visualized as collapsing/folding
- Examples: torus, Klein bottle, projective plane, real projective line
- Universal property of quotient maps

## Content Outline

### Scene 1: Hook — "Gluing Spaces Together" (~50s)
Introduce the idea of identifying points. Visual: taking a strip and gluing ends to make a circle.

### Scene 2: Equivalence Relations and Quotient Sets (~60s)
Define equivalence relation on X, quotient set X/~. Visual: partitioning a set into equivalence classes.

### Scene 3: The Quotient Topology (~70s)
Definition: finest topology making q: X -> X/~ continuous. Open sets in X/~ are those whose preimage is open in X. Visual: map from X collapsing to quotient.

### Scene 4: Classic Example — The Circle (~60s)
[0,1] with 0 ~ 1 gives S^1. Visual: interval with endpoints identified.

### Scene 5: The Torus as a Quotient (~70s)
R^2 / Z^2 or [0,1]^2 with edge identifications. Visual: square with arrows. First pair opposite edges (cylinder), then the other pair (torus).

### Scene 6: The Klein Bottle and Projective Plane (~60s)
Klein bottle: twist one pair of identifications. Projective plane: identify antipodal points. Visual: square diagrams with arrows.
