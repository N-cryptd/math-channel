## Key Insights

### What Works Across Competitors
1. **The domino/ladder analogy is universal** — Every video uses some version of this. It's the single most effective way to introduce induction.
2. **Progression: intuition first, then formality** — The best videos (Zach Star, Trefor) build visual intuition before writing the formal P(k) → P(k+1) structure.
3. **Worked examples are essential** — Abstract induction is confusing. A concrete example (sum formula) makes it click.
4. **Color-coding the induction hypothesis** — Highlighting P(k) in a distinct color from P(k+1) helps viewers track the logical flow.
5. **Explicit labeling of the three steps** — "Base case, Inductive hypothesis, Inductive step" should be clearly labeled, not just implied.

### What Doesn't Work
1. **Philosophical digressions** (Numberphile) — Spending 10+ minutes on "what induction feels like" without teaching the technique loses students who need the method.
2. **Jumping straight to formalism** (Kimberly Brehm) — Without the domino/ladder visual first, the formal statement is dry and unmotivated.
3. **PowerPoint slides** (Kimberly Brehm) — Static slides feel dated. Animated content (even simple) is now expected.
4. **Weak connection to the Well-Ordering Principle** — When mentioned, it often confuses rather than clarifies. Keep it brief or omit.

### Distinctive Approaches Worth Noting
- **Zach Star's grid visualization** for the sum formula — showing 1+2+...+n as a growing triangle/grid, then showing n(n+1)/2 as the area. This is a beautiful visual proof that complements the algebraic induction proof.
- **Numberphile's set theory framing** — Induction as "you can't check infinitely many things, so you need a shortcut." This motivation is good for the hook even if the execution is too long.
- **Trefor's learning objectives** — Explicitly stating what the viewer will learn at the start is good practice for educational content.

## Techniques to Adopt in Our Video

1. **Animated domino chain** — Use Manim to show dominoes toppling one by one. Color the "base case" domino (first) and the "inductive step" connection between adjacent dominoes. This is the strongest opening.
2. **Three-phase structure** — (1) Domino visual + "why we need this" hook, (2) Formal induction principle with clear labeling, (3) Worked example with color-coded P(k) → P(k+1).
3. **Grid visualization** for 1+2+...+n = n(n+1)/2 — Show the triangle growing, then duplicate and rearrange to show the area. This is our unique visual contribution beyond what competitors show.
4. **Color-code consistently** — P(k) in PRIMARY (blue), P(k+1) in SECONDARY (green), the substitution step in ACCENT (yellow), base case verified in SECONDARY.
5. **Strong induction section** — Brief but clear distinction. Show "assume ALL previous" vs "assume only k" with a visual metaphor (full staircase foundation vs single rung support).
6. **Explicit "when to use induction" signals** — Following Video 93's pattern, list trigger words: "for all n ≥ 1", "prove by induction", "n!", sum/product formulas.

## Techniques to Avoid or Adapt

1. **Numberphile's no-visuals approach** — Our Manim channel should animate everything. No paper-and-pen.
2. **Kimberly Brehm's Well-Ordering Principle detour** — Keep the focus on induction mechanics. Mention WOP briefly in a note, not as a main section.
3. **Over-reliance on one example** — Zach Star does only one example. We should do 2 (sum formula + one inequality) plus mention strong induction.
4. **Lecture-style delivery** — Even formal proofs should feel like a story, not a lecture.
