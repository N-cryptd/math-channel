### Key Insights
- Group actions are abstract; every successful video grounds them in concrete examples FIRST
- The permutation action on a set is the universal example everyone uses
- 3B1B's approach of leading with a big mystery is the gold standard for engagement
- Cayley diagrams (Visual Algebra) are an underused visual that we could adopt
- The orbit-stabilizer theorem is usually the "aha moment" — it connects actions to group structure
- Most competitors spend too long on formal verification of the axioms

### Techniques to Adopt
- Open with a concrete symmetry example (rotations of a square/triangle) to motivate the definition
- Visualize orbits as paths through Cayley diagrams (adopted from Visual Algebra)
- Use the orbit-stabilizer theorem as the dramatic payoff
- Color-code: group elements in one color, set elements in another
- Tease Cayley's theorem and Sylow theorems as future content (cliffhanger)

### Techniques to Avoid
- Don't combine group actions + Cayley's theorem + symmetric group in one video (Essence of Group Theory does this — too much)
- Don't spend >2 minutes verifying the axioms formally (most competitors waste time here)
- Avoid purely blackboard/lecture format (Visual Group Theory 5.1)

### Standout Approaches
- 3B1B's narrative structure (mystery -> tools -> reveal) is the model
- Visual Algebra's "five features" checklist provides clear learning objectives
- The orbit-stabilizer theorem as the "so what" moment is universal across good treatments
