## Key Insights

### What Works
1. **Historical narrative hooks** — Mathologer's framing of Ptolemy's theorem within ancient astronomy makes it memorable
2. **Animated geometric proofs** — The scaling/alignment technique is visually compelling
3. **Color-coding** — Consistent use of colors for different elements in diagrams
4. **Chapter breaks** — Timestamped sections help viewers navigate

### What Doesn't Work
1. **Excessive length** — 31 minutes for a single theorem is too long for most viewers
2. **Getting lost in tangents** — Historical asides can derail the main narrative
3. **Lack of scaffolding** — Mathologer assumes significant prior knowledge
4. **No "meta" discussion of proof techniques** — The videos present individual proofs but don't teach HOW to prove

## Techniques to Adopt in Our Video

1. **Hook with a surprising counterexample** — Euler's n² + n + 41 is perfect; it viscerally demonstrates why examples aren't proofs
2. **Animated checkmarks → single red X** — Visual pattern that matches the emotional arc (confidence → surprise)
3. **Color-coded proof steps** — Each logical step gets a color, making the chain of reasoning visible
4. **"Anatomy of a proof" framing** — Break down WHAT a proof is before showing one
5. **Preview cards for techniques** — Small visual previews of proof methods to build anticipation for the playlist

## Techniques to Avoid or Adapt

1. **Mathologer's length** — Keep our video under 12 minutes. One topic, tight focus.
2. **Pure symbolic proofs (Math Sorcerer style)** — Always lead with intuition, follow with rigor
3. **Dense theorem statements** — No theorem appears without motivation
4. **Skipping the "why"** — Every proof technique must answer: "When would I use this?"

## Standout Approaches

- **Mathologer's historical narrative** — Weaving history into math is powerful but must be brief
- **3B1B's color-coding consistency** — Every video in a series should use the same color language
- **Reducible's simplicity** — Clean, uncluttered visuals; one idea at a time

## Recommendations for Video 90

1. **Title:** "Why Do We Need Proofs?" (curiosity gap)
2. **Opening:** Start with the n² + n + 41 counterexample — 40 green checkmarks, then BAM, red X at 40
3. **Structure:** Hook → What is a proof? → Anatomy → Types preview → First direct proof → Outro
4. **Visual metaphor:** A proof as a chain — each link is a logical step. Break one link, the chain fails.
5. **Color scheme:** Green = verified, Red = counterexample, Blue = logical step, Yellow = conclusion
6. **Outro:** "Next: Direct Proof — the simplest and most common proof technique"
